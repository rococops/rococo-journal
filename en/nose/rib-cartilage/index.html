Autologous Rib Cartilage Rhinoplasty

How is rib cartilage rhinoplasty done?

Of ear cartilage, septal cartilage and rib cartilage, the one least likely to bend when used to raise the nasal tip is rib cartilage. That is, assuming every material is used well.

  • 15minRib cartilage harvest and closure
  • 2cmIncision length at the harvest site
  • 90–150minSurgery time (upturned nose correction · combined surgery)

DOCTOR'S NOTE

Dr. Kim's notes

Why I use only autologous rib cartilage

Until 4–5 years ago, I also used donor rib cartilage from time to time. That was because I could understand patients' fear? anxiety? about having rib cartilage taken from their side.

However, through long-term direct and indirect experience of what became of the many donor rib cartilages used in revision rhinoplasty, for contracted noses, upturned noses and so on, I came to see quite often that a fair number of donor rib cartilages were partly or almost completely absorbed in the nasal tip, so that the tip turned up again or dropped and revision surgery was needed.

Of course, it is possible that I have judged donor rib cartilage more negatively because the people who came to me for autologous rib cartilage rhinoplasty were those who had problems after donor rib cartilage surgery at other clinics.

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

Does the nose bend if the tip is raised with rib cartilage?

Of course, raising the nasal tip with autologous rib cartilage is no 100% guarantee that the nose will not bend. This is because a bending nose is a very complex problem, not just a matter of the tip cartilage. In any case, comparing only the autologous cartilages used to raise the tip, the chance of the nose bending with rib cartilage is ve~ry low compared with the other cartilages.

However, as someone who uses ear, septal and rib cartilage widely and evenly, I will say it once more with confidence based on ample experience: " When the nasal tip is raised with rib cartilage, the chance of the nose bending is the lowest. "

There are several reasons why rib cartilage bends the least of the three cartilages, and the key point is that <rib cartilage is large and firm>.

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

Silicone for the bridge, rib cartilage for the tip

When I do rhinoplasty with rib cartilage, in most cases I use rib cartilage only for the nasal tip.

These days.. I use a silicone implant as much as possible, and build the bridge with rib cartilage only when I judge that an implant really cannot be used.

The reason is that when I compare silicone implants vs. autologous rib cartilage as materials for raising the bridge, both have pros and cons, but in my experience I judged that silicone implants have more advantages, and I still firmly hold that view.

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

14 years of rib cartilage harvest

I started rib cartilage rhinoplasty in 2008, so this year marks a full 14 years of harvesting autologous rib cartilage.

In those 14 years, there have been very occasional cases where a noticeable scar remained at the rib cartilage harvest site and I managed it with scar revision or injections, but I have never once received a complaint from a patient about a scar problem.

METHOD

Surgical method

  1. STEP 01

    First, after harvesting the autologous rib cartilage, the cartilage is trimmed and soaked in saline.

  2. STEP 02

    If it is already bent before soaking, hatching incisions (scoring lines) that help straighten it are made before it is stored in saline.

  3. STEP 03

    The nose is opened and disassembled, removing the old implant, previously grafted cartilage and so on. Revision rhinoplasty is a process of taking apart and reassembling.

  4. STEP 04

    Opening the nose takes about 10 to 30 minutes, and in the meantime the warping of the rib cartilage soaked in saline is completed.

  5. STEP 05

    Using various know-how, I make straight rib cartilage struts to build the nasal tip.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →

I raise the tip using two layers of thinly trimmed rib cartilage; usually in about 70% the rib cartilage hardly bends, and in about 30% warping occurs.

The biggest advantage of donor rib cartilage is that it avoids the risks that can arise when rib cartilage is taken from our own body. The more experience the surgeon has in harvesting autologous rib cartilage, the greater the advantage of using autologous rib cartilage over donor rib cartilage.

It is simply that I have the know-how to obtain autologous rib cartilage safely through a small wound, and in my experience I think autologous rib cartilage is much better than donor rib cartilage in many ways, so I use only autologous rib cartilage. For someone who is extremely reluctant to have a scar on the body, I think donor rib cartilage can be an alternative to autologous rib cartilage despite a few drawbacks. In particular, when it is used not in an area that must bear a lot of force, such as raising the tip or bringing down an upturned nose, but to raise the bridge or for spreader grafts and the like, I think donor rib cartilage is good value given the advantage of leaving no scar compared with autologous rib cartilage.

RECOVERY

Recovery

  1. Upturned nose correction

    Surgery time: 1 hour 30 minutes – 2 hours. Recovery period: 1 week. Face washing and makeup: from 1 week.

  2. Combined surgery

    With surgery like this, the operation usually takes about 2 hours 30 minutes, and you can think of the recovery period as about 2 weeks.

    For 1 week after surgery, skin-colored paper tape stays on, and after 1 week you can wash your face and wear makeup.

  3. Harvest site, 1 month

    This is the scar one month after surgery. It is an average scar. Some people get a darker scar, and in others it becomes a very thin white line that is hard to see.

  4. Swelling

    You can see that there is still swelling on the nasal tip. This swelling keeps being absorbed for at least 1 year, and the shape improves.

Frequently asked questions

Does the nose bend if the tip is raised with rib cartilage?

Without a second thought, I would answer: " When rhinoplasty is done with rib cartilage, the chance of the nose bending is the lowest. How much of a scar is left where the rib cartilage was taken?

A 2 cm linear scar remains on the side of the body, and it fades a lot over time. I have harvested rib cartilage from a great many people over a long time, but I have never received any particular complaint about the scar. It is usually noticeable because of the dark color, and this pigmentation keeps fading over 1–2 years.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
Is rib cartilage harvest safe?

I make the incision about halfway between the lower chest line and the pelvic bone, and mainly use the 9th rib cartilage, sometimes the 8th. The advantage of the 9th–8th rib cartilages over the 5th–6th is that there is almost no chance of the complication called pneumothorax during harvest. Rib cartilage can be obtained much more safely.
