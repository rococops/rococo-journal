TYPE · HUMP NOSE

A hump nose has two features

  • First, a convex bump that sticks out on the bridge of the nose (the hump), and
  • second, a tip that droops downward, so that from the front it looks like an arrow-shaped nose.

Hump noses are often crooked as well. Because the hump hides the deviation, surgery is sometimes done without noticing it, which often leads to revision surgery. That is why careful diagnosis and planning are needed before surgery.

DOCTOR'S NOTE

Dr. Kim's notes

Why does a hump nose form?

When a baby's face is forming in the womb and the nose is taking shape, the septal cartilage should grow just enough inside the bony space of the nose (the nasal cavity). I believe a hump forms because the cartilage keeps growing after it has already filled that space, bulging outward onto the bridge of the nose.

What this means clinically is that the over-grown (?) septal cartilage doesn't only push up into the bridge to make a hump. It also pushes downward to create an arrow-shaped nose, and bends sideways to create a crooked nose.

In the end, with most hump noses we find that "when you open the nose, the septum is badly bent."

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

Implant or no implant?

Broadly, a hump nose is corrected either with an implant or without one. Neither method is better in itself, because not all hump noses are the same. The best method is the one most suited to your own nose.

In my experience, roughly half of the people who come to me about a hump nose have a hump nose that should not be treated with an implant.

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

METHOD

Surgical steps

  1. STEP 01

    Under sedation

  2. STEP 02

    Straightening the deviation with septal cartilage

  3. STEP 03

    Raising the nasal tip

  4. STEP 04

    Smoothing down the hump

  5. STEP 05

    Filling the hollow between the bridge and the tip, again with septal cartilage.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →

Depending on the nose, this basic procedure may also include osteotomy, and ear cartilage or MegaDerm may be added.

The biggest advantage of implant-free hump correction is, quite literally, that no implant is used. Being able to raise the nose without an implant is a huge advantage. Not leaving any foreign material in your body is the greatest benefit in itself, and the risk of infection is also much lower than with implant surgery.

However, no matter how the surgery is done, it will not create a bridge line as smooth as that of a nose with an implant.

RECOVERY

Recovery

  1. Day of surgery

    Under sedation,

    the surgery takes about 1 hour.

  2. 1 week

    For one week after surgery, only skin-colored tape is placed on the nose,

    and after a week the tape is removed and you can wash your face and wear makeup.

  3. 2 weeks

    Bruising and swelling can last about 2 weeks after surgery.

    So you can think of the standard recovery period as 2 weeks.

  4. Residual swelling

    (Subtle residual swelling keeps going down for up to a year and a half.)

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →

For typical nose surgery, we advise a recovery period of 2 weeks in 99% of cases, and 4 weeks or more in 1%.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an implant for hump nose surgery?

If there is enough height between the eyebrows (the glabella), a hump nose can be corrected without an implant. If the glabella is low and a good line can't be made, an implant has to be used.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
I heard the hump grows back unless silicone is put in. Is that true?

A hump does not grow. That claim has no basis at all; think of it simply as a difference in surgical experience and technique.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
Will some contour remain on the bridge if no implant is used?

The biggest drawback of the implant-free method is that some contour remains along the bridge. Personally I think a moderate contour is actually an advantage because it makes the nose look natural, but if much of the cartilage grafted onto the bridge is absorbed and more contour appears than expected, an additional correction to add volume may be needed after 6 months.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
My nose has a hump. Do I also need the deviation corrected?

You can assume that 99% of hump noses are crooked.
