Closed Rhinoplasty (Scarless Rhinoplasty)

How is closed (scarless) rhinoplasty done?

This method applies only when a silicone implant is used: the silicone is placed somewhat long, and ear cartilage is placed on the nasal tip.

ADVANTAGES · CLOSED RHINOPLASTY

This surgery has quite a lot of advantages.

  • Being a closed technique, it leaves no external scar
  • The nostrils barely change from the front (with septal or rib cartilage, the nostrils get pinched and pulled up to some degree)
  • Because the surgery does not touch the internal structures of the nose (such as the septum) at all, there is no chance of breathing discomfort after surgery
  • The nasal tip does not get lower over the years
  • It can create a naturally high nose
  • After surgery you can still push the tip up to some extent, as in making a "pig nose"

As I mentioned in an earlier post, quite a lot of people come to Rococo because of a retracted columella, and in many cases I recommend this closed silicone + ear cartilage surgery to people with a retracted columella.

METHOD

Surgical steps

  1. STEP 01

    The closed rhinoplasty I perform uses silicone and ear cartilage.

  2. STEP 02

    The silicone is placed long,

  3. STEP 03

    and the nasal tip is wrapped with ear cartilage, so unlike the usual L-shaped silicone method, there is no chance of the silicone implant poking through the skin.

  4. STEP 04

    Although the columella was not lowered, leftover ear cartilage was placed in the columella to gain a little volume.

  5. STEP 05

    Swelling of the nasal tip keeps going down for up to 1 year and 6 months afterward.

After surgery, the patient's own tip cartilage can be pushed down slightly and felt inside the nostril, but as the swelling goes down between 6 and 12 months it goes back in a lot, and I have never seen it become a problem.

Its relative disadvantages are:

It cannot raise the tip as sharply and as much as rib cartilage

It cannot lower the columella

It cannot lift a downward-drooping, arrow-shaped tip up toward the glabella (between the eyebrows)

It cannot raise only the tip without silicone

Recovery

  1. Day of surgery

    Anesthesia: sedation

    Surgery time: about 1 hour

  2. 5 days

    Face washing and makeup: from day 5

    With Rococo rhinoplasty alone, the swelling goes down enough by 5 days after surgery that you can go back to work.

  3. 1 week

    Recovery period: 1 week

  4. 1–2 weeks (with hump correction / osteotomy)

    In surgery like this, the surgery time is about 40 minutes and the recovery period is about 1–2 weeks.

  5. 1 year 6 months

    Swelling of the nasal tip keeps going down for up to 1 year and 6 months afterward.

It is the least aggressive and least destructive surgery, and accordingly it is safe and recovery is quick.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can the tip be raised a lot with the closed method?

It cannot raise the tip a lot in a noticeable way. It cannot lower the columella much. It can give a little volume.

Won't breathing become uncomfortable after surgery?

This surgery is different from the general L-shaped silicone method, and because it does not touch the internal structures of the nose at all, there are no problems such as breathing discomfort after surgery.

Over the years, won't the tip get lower or the cartilage show through?

The tip does not get lower over the years. As a trade-off for the tip not getting lower, there is a possibility that the tip cartilage may show through as the years pass. But that possibility is very low.

Can a pinched nose also be corrected with the closed method?

Correcting a pinched nose is also a surgery I perform quite often. When it is done as a standalone surgery, I usually correct it through an incision inside the nose (closed) by inserting donor dermis. No scar is left on the outside, and although the surgery is relatively simple, the correction is effective, so it is one of the surgeries with quite high patient satisfaction.

If cartilage is removed to correct a scar inside the nose, won't the tip collapse?

Many people ask, "If you remove the cartilage in that area, won't the tip collapse?" but that is not the case. It is the same principle as a pillar with thorns sticking out on its side: smoothing down the thorns does not make the pillar collapse.
