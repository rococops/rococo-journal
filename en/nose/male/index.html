DESIGN · MALE NOSE

The biggest difference between male and female rhinoplasty is the angle of the nasal tip.

  • A feminine tip should be slightly upturned, while a male tip should project almost perpendicular to the face, straight out, to give off masculine appeal.
  • The glabella is also designed a little higher than for women, and
  • the bridge is designed to run smoothly in a straight line rather than a slightly scooped curve.

This is the most typical male rhinoplasty case. The glabella is low and the tip droops. Augmentation rhinoplasty was performed using septal cartilage from inside the nose and a silicone implant.

Is a male hump nose different from a female one?

Is there a difference between a male hump nose vs. a female hump nose? In fact, there is no particular difference. The principles of the surgery are the same.

However, men tend to have thicker, tougher skin and a somewhat greater tendency to bleed than women. Because the skin is tough, surgery is a little harder in every case compared with women, whose skin is generally soft.

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

The biggest cause of a crooked nose is facial asymmetry

Over many years of practice I must have performed countless crooked nose surgeries.. From that experience, what I consider the biggest cause of a crooked nose is <facial asymmetry>. The base the nose sits on, that is, the central axis of the face, is twisted, so the nose in the middle of the face cannot help but be crooked.

In my experience, the most common facial asymmetry in East Asians.. is the central axis of the philtrum being rotated to the right relative to the central axis of the glabella. I think roughly 80% are twisted to the right and roughly 20% to the left.

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

Limits of a crooked nose when the facial axis is twisted

There is no way to correct a bend in the upper part of the bridge. Aligning the tip to the uppermost center point of the bridge is the way to make the nose look the least crooked.

It would be nice to have it all, but there is not yet any medical technology that can straighten a twisted facial axis, so operating that way is the best we can do for now.

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

METHOD

Surgical method

  1. STEP 01

    Hump nose correction and rhinitis surgery were performed under sedation.

  2. STEP 02

    The surgery is done implant-free: the tip is raised with septal cartilage,

  3. STEP 03

    the hump is slightly smoothed down,

  4. STEP 04

    and the areas above and below the hump are filled in with septal cartilage.

  5. STEP 05

    Cartilage was also grafted on the left side of the bridge to correct the crooked look, while making up for a bridge that felt too narrow (a knife-edge nose).

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →

If septal cartilage is insufficient, ear cartilage or AlloDerm can be used to fill in.

In addition, to overcome the facial asymmetry, I used a method called the swing door technique, which pushes the tip to the left. The drawbacks are: (1) the columella becomes bent, (2) nostril asymmetry develops — when pushed to the left, the left nostril sits higher than the right nostril from the front, and (3) when the tip is pushed to the left, breathing through the left nostril can become a little easier and breathing through the right nostril a little harder.

Despite these drawbacks, having the bridge look closer to straight from the front is the bigger gain, so I correct crooked noses this way.

RECOVERY

After surgery

  1. Swelling

    As the swelling goes down, the tip becomes slimmer and more defined.

  2. 3 months

    This photo is 3 months after surgery. Because the tip was pushed to the left (the right side of the photo), it is clearly closer to straight than before surgery. It does not become perfectly straight. A slight crooked look remains.

  3. Breathing

    Ve~ry rarely, if breathing discomfort caused by (3) lasts more than 3 months, the tip position can be restored to its original state after discussing it with the patient. Breathing would then improve again, but the nose would look crooked.

  4. 1 year

    To properly see the result you need photos after 1 year, but when there are no problems with the surgery, patients don't come back for check-ups.. so for now I'm showing at least these early progress photos.

  5. If the tip was raised a lot

    I would raise it a bit higher with autologous rib cartilage, but the higher the tip becomes, the greater the chance of the tip cartilage showing through afterward, and if the cartilage show becomes severe, corrective surgery to lower the tip is needed.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

If I have a deviated septum, does it have to be straightened?

No one has a perfectly straight space where the septum sits. It is bound to be twisted, even slightly. So there is no case where the septum itself is perfectly straight. That is because a perfectly symmetrical face doesn't exist.. In other words, strictly speaking, everyone in the world has a deviated septum. Many people vaguely think.. "I have a deviated septum, so it has to be straightened for my nose to be straight.." but this really applies only in some cases.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
My nostrils are M-shaped. What happens if the tip is raised?

The nostrils are slightly M-shaped, and since the plan is to raise the tip with a septal extension using septal cartilage, I let the patient know in advance that the M shape will become a little more pronounced after surgery. Those who don't want that can have alar rim lowering at the same time. At the current degree it isn't essential, so I explain it to the patient at the consultation and let them decide for themselves.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
If a hump is corrected without an implant, won't irregularities remain?

In implant-free hump nose surgery, it is common for the area between the tip and the hump to look sunken in the left oblique photo after surgery. This problem occurs when the volume along the sides of the bridge is not filled enough; there is a limit to some degree, but the line has been shaped as much as possible.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
What methods are there for hump nose correction?

Hump nose correction can be done with an implant, or without an implant using only your own tissue.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
Can a male nose with a retracted columella be corrected?

A retracted columella is difficult to operate on and the results have limits, so I recommend a <silicone + ear cartilage> surgery that simply raises the nose without columella lowering.
