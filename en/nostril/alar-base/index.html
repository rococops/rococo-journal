TYPE · ALAR BASE

To get the full effect of alar base reduction, the ala first needs to be fairly thin.

  • The thicker the ala, the weaker the effect.
  • Seen from below, the ala should flare out in a somewhat rounded shape.
  • Among people whose nostrils show a lot from the front, in those with large nostrils this surgery can also greatly reduce nostril show.

Nostril reduction, which makes the nostrils smaller, is directly linked to breathing function, so the most important thing is to always design it so that it feels slightly too little. Because it cannot be reversed, it is a simple surgery but can be a very risky one.

Often done together with bulbous tip correction

Many people come to the clinic for bulbous tip correction. Bulbous tip correction is generally a surgery that reduces the size of the nasal tip, but when the tip becomes smaller, the alae attached on either side often look larger. So in many cases alar base reduction is done together with bulbous tip correction.

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

Alae for which I don't recommend alar base reduction

Among people who ask about alar base reduction, there are many for whom I advise against surgery. Most of them have narrow nostrils at the floor and almost no slack on the lower outer side of the ala.

Alar base reduction in particular is simple, but it cannot be reversed and can be a very critical surgery.

The problem is that there is no way to undo it with revision surgery.

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

Where the incision goes, and suturing

If you look at the photo, the alar base reduction incision is located exactly at the point where the ala meets the cheek and bends. Most people assume that since this area is a crease, of course the incision should be made there (that's where to cut) to leave less of a scar. In reality, if you make the incision there, the two cut surfaces don't meet in parallel but have to join at an angle, bent, so it is hard for the suture line to heal neatly. In the end, that makes a scar more likely.

To put it simply, the key to suturing is to join the two surfaces well with the least possible tension.

So more and tighter stitches are not always better! It's good to keep that in mind.

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

Surgery that minimizes scars starts with the design

The most important point of alar reduction surgery, as I see it, is <minimizing the scar>.

Surgery that minimizes scars starts with the design. The size of the ala is a given, and the design must also take into account whether the skin is oily or dry, the elasticity of the ala, and the shape of the ala when the client smiles; during surgery, the angle and depth at which the scalpel enters, hemostasis after removal, the depth of the sutures, the type and thickness of the thread, and so on..

No matter who in the world performs the surgery, a scar remains. But I believe the essence of alar reduction surgery is to operate so that the scar is as unnoticeable as possible.

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

METHOD

Surgical method

  1. STEP 01

    In type A, with small nostrils, an elliptical excision, the most common method for alar base reduction, is performed.

  2. STEP 02

    Because only the ala is reduced, the nostril hardly changes.

  3. STEP 03

    In patients with thick alae, surgery to reduce the width of the ala can be done at the same time, but with this surgery a slight scar may be visible along the inner border of the nostril.

  4. STEP 04

    In type B, with large nostrils, nostril reduction (making the nostrils smaller) can be done together with alar base reduction.

  5. STEP 05

    In type D, the nostrils are reduced while the alae are drawn together with Elasticum thread. This gives the greatest effect.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →

This usual form of alar base reduction is meaningful only for alae of types A & B. If this kind of surgery is done for C & D, it only leaves a scar, and it is hard to feel any reduction of the ala itself.

No matter how well the surgery is done, a scar will always remain. But the surgery must be done so that, after 1–2 years when the dark red color of the scar has faded, it becomes as unnoticeable a scar as possible.

For reference, these days I rarely perform non-incisional alar base reduction using thread on its own; I only do it for people who want it as an add-on when having nostril-ala reduction. This is because, looking at long-term results over 5 years or more, I judged that the tendency to relapse is high.

RECOVERY

Recovery

  1. 5 days

    Recovery period: 5 days

    Face washing and makeup: from day 5

  2. After stitch removal

    After the stitches are removed the scar is still slightly noticeable, but after 6–12 months only a very faint line remains and it becomes hard to see.

  3. 6–12 months

    Over 6–12 months this scar fades considerably, to the point that it is almost invisible unless you look closely. Of course, how visible the scar is will vary slightly depending on constitution.

  4. If your skin is darker

    For people with darker skin, like this client and like me, there is more melanin pigment when the body is wounded, so the scar tends to be more noticeable.

    But even in this case, all scars fade over time.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

If I only have bulbous tip correction, will my alae look bigger?

If only the bulbous tip is corrected without working on the alae, the alae can look relatively larger after surgery, so for noses like this it is better to have both done together.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
Does alar base reduction leave a scar?

No matter how well the surgery is done, a scar will always remain. But the surgery must be done so that, after 1–2 years when the dark red color of the scar has faded, it becomes as unnoticeable a scar as possible.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
If the nostrils are reduced too, won't breathing become uncomfortable?

Of course, because the nostrils become smaller, there is a theoretical possibility that nasal breathing may become uncomfortable, so we take this fully into account and decide together how much to reduce the nostrils.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
Is there anything to be careful about after surgery?

Smoking is the worst thing for wound healing in particular. The reason is that it reduces peripheral blood circulation, so more of a scar remains.
