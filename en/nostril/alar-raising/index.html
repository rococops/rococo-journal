TYPE · ALAR RAISING

Alar rim lifting is needed in cases such as

  • When both alae droop and the columella is raised so the tip looks a bit like a "pig nose" (this is how patients describe it): the columella is lowered while both alae are lifted at the same time
  • When facial asymmetry makes only one ala droop
  • This can even create an optical illusion that the nose is crooked, so the surgery is sometimes done to correct that as well.

The usual methods are not very effective, so over a long time I have gradually developed the technique. It has become my own alar rim lifting method.

Third- and fourth-generation alar rim lifting

The alar rim lifting I mainly perform is third-generation alar rim lifting. Because it is done from inside the nostril, there is no scar on the outside, and it is mainly used to reduce nostril show from the front (V-nostril correction) or to lift an ala that hangs down like a hammock in the side profile.

With third-generation alar rim lifting, the corner of the ala hardly rises, so after thinking it over I created fourth-generation alar rim lifting. The fourth-generation method uses an incision on the outer skin side, and because the corner of the ala is fully lifted, it can create a more noticeable change.

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

A surgery to reduce nostril show from the front

The main purpose of alar rim lifting is to reduce nostril show from the front, so there is not much change from the side or at an angle.

How much the nostril is reduced with third-generation alar rim lifting depends on the angle of the nostril floor, the size of the nostril, the condition of the tissue around the nostril and so on, and I don't recommend surgery in every case. I recommend it only when some degree of change can be expected.

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

A drooping ala in side profile can also be corrected

If you have had philtrum reduction

Alar rim lifting works by pulling up tissue from the philtrum area, so in people who have had philtrum reduction there is less slack in the philtrum area, and the effect is inevitably weaker.

This is a surgery I privately call second-generation alar rim lifting. Compared with the first-generation method, there may be slightly~ more swelling around the lip, which could be a small drawback(?), but the swelling hardly interferes with daily life. The advantages are that the ala rises even a little more and the chance of leaving a scar is reduced.

— Dr. Kim Sang-ho

METHOD

Surgical method

  1. STEP 01

    The surgery is done under sedation, so there is no pain at all,

  2. STEP 02

    and it takes about 20–30 minutes.

  3. STEP 03

    No separate tape is applied,

  4. STEP 04

    and the stitches inside the nose are kept as invisible as possible.

  5. STEP 05

    The stitches inside the nose are removed 1 week after surgery.

Alar rim lifting and philtrum reduction pull tissue in opposite directions, so I don't recommend doing them at the same time.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →

As for the drawbacks of alar rim lifting: in patients who have had nostril reduction or philtrum reduction, the effect is inevitably weaker.

The nostrils may become narrower after surgery, and this is especially likely in patients who have previously had nostril reduction. If the nostrils become narrower, breathing could theoretically become uncomfortable.

A scar forms inside the nostril. Most are hard to notice, but very rarely the scar becomes raised and visible. In such cases we manage it as much as possible with scar injections and similar treatment.

RECOVERY

Recovery

  1. Day of surgery

    No tape or anything like that is applied after surgery, swelling is relatively mild as you can see in the photos, and I have not yet seen anyone bruise after this surgery.

  2. Next day

    Most patients say they go about their daily lives from the next day without much discomfort.

  3. 1 week

    The stitches inside the nose are removed 1 week after surgery.

  4. Swelling

    To reduce scarring as much as possible, the area of tissue dissection is wider, so there tends to be somewhat more swelling after surgery than with the first-generation surgery.

  5. Scar

    In most cases the scar inside the nostril is barely visible after surgery, but occasionally a noticeable scar remains.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can alar rim lifting and philtrum reduction be done at the same time?

Alar rim lifting and philtrum reduction pull tissue in opposite directions, so I don't recommend doing them at the same time.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
Can the nostrils become narrower or breathing become uncomfortable after surgery?

In most cases the shape of the nostrils does not change, but rarely the nostrils become narrower as the wound heals. So for people whose nostrils are already quite small or narrow, I explain this possibility before surgery and let them know that, in the unlikely event the nostrils narrow, breathing could become uncomfortable.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
Will a scar show inside the nostril after surgery?

In most cases the scar inside the nostril is barely visible after surgery, but occasionally a noticeable scar remains.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
How much are the nostrils covered?

The nostril rises by about 1–2 mm. The triangular nostril at the upper left remains, but as nostril show is reduced it becomes a little less noticeable.

From Dr. Kim's column (Korean) →
Does fourth-generation alar rim lifting leave a scar on the outside?

This method leaves a scar on the skin side, but it fades a lot over 1–2 years; so far there have been no particular complaints, and patient satisfaction with this surgery is high.
